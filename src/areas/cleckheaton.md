Cleckheaton, Gomersal, Liversedge and Heckmondwike are Spen Valley stone — terraces and mill housing in gritstone, laid in lime, with the odd later brick street. The tell-tale in the Spen Valley is a wall that has been cement-pointed three times and is still damp inside; the stone is holding water because the joints won't let it out.

We take the cement out by hand and repoint in lime with a matched aggregate. Gomersal has a conservation area. Fifty minutes from Rochdale on the M62, grouped with Huddersfield, Halifax and Dewsbury work.

A typical Spen Valley job is a full front on a Bradford Road or Westgate terrace, or a gable-and-chimney together on one of the exposed hillside streets towards Hunsworth and Scholes. The bigger Victorian houses in Gomersal and around Oakenshaw have carved dressings and deep joints for a slightly recessed lime finish.
