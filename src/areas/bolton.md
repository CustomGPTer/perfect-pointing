Bolton is at the edge of our usual range but we're there regularly. Stone terraces and cottages around Bromley Cross, Egerton, Edgworth and Harwood want lime; the Victorian brick of Halliwell, Tonge Moor and Great Lever, and the 1930s semis across Heaton and Smithills, are generally cement. Bigger jobs — a full elevation, a couple of chimneys — are well worth the drive; for a single small patch, ring and ask.

What we see most in Bolton is Accrington and common red brick that was repointed in a strong sand-and-cement mix in the 70s and 80s. It sits proud of the face, holds water against the arris, and the brick fails before the mortar does — frost-spalled faces on north gables around Astley Bridge and Breightmet are typical. The fix is to cut out the worst bricks, replace them with matches, then rake and repoint the elevation in a mortar the brick can cope with.

Up on the moor edge at Egerton, Edgworth and Belmont the stone terraces get the full weather. We rake the joints back to sound mortar, repoint in a lime mix matched to the original colour and keep the work sheeted until it has cured — a rushed lime job on an exposed wall up here will not last a winter.
