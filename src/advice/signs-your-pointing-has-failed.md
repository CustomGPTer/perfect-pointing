Pointing doesn't fail overnight; it erodes, then it lets water in, then the damage moves to the brick and the inside of the house. Catching it at stage one is a lot cheaper than stage three.

1. **Crumbling or missing mortar.** Run a key along a joint. If it powders or you can push it back more than a few millimetres, it's gone.
2. **Joints recessed behind the brick face.** Weathered-out joints leave a ledge on top of every brick. Water sits there.
3. **Spalled brick faces.** Flakes or whole faces missing from the brick, usually on a north or west elevation. This almost always means hard cement over soft brick — see [lime or cement](/advice/lime-or-cement-mortar/).
4. **Green staining or moss in the joints.** Moss needs water to grow. If the joints are hosting it, they're holding water.
5. **Damp patches inside** on an outside wall or around a chimney breast, with no plumbing nearby. Pointing or flaunching is the usual cause.
6. **A hollow sound** when you tap the mortar. Old repointing that was smeared over rather than raked out has come away from the wall behind it.

## What to do

Photograph it and send it to us. Most of these can be priced roughly from a couple of photos, and Scott will tell you if it's a single-wall job or a whole-house job. Don't let anyone talk you into "sealing" the wall — sealants trap moisture in a wall that's already wet.
