"Repointing required to external walls" is one of the most common lines on a Level 2 or Level 3 survey in Greater Manchester, because most houses round here are over eighty years old and most have never been repointed properly. It's rarely a reason to walk away. It is a reason to get a real price rather than guess.

## What the surveyor means

Surveyors look from the ground and flag what they see: eroded joints, cement patching, spalled bricks, a chimney that looks tired. They'll usually grade it — condition rating 2 (defects that need repair but aren't urgent) or 3 (serious, needs attention now). Rating 2 on pointing is normal for a pre-war house. Rating 3, or any mention of damp inside linked to the walls, means water is already getting in and the job goes to the top of the list.

What the survey won't tell you is the area involved or the cost. "Repointing required" can mean one gable or the whole house.

## If you're buying

1. **Get a proper quote before exchange.** Send us the survey and photos of each elevation, or ask for a survey visit — we'll give a fixed price by elevation so you know exactly what you're dealing with. That turns a vague worry into a number.
2. **Negotiate with the number.** A typical semi needing a full repoint is a few thousand pounds; a gable and a chimney is a lot less. Sellers will usually knock off a fair share of a written quote; they won't move much for "the survey says the pointing's bad".
3. **Don't ignore rating 3.** Water coming in through the walls damages plaster, timber and decoration, and the bill grows. Budget to have it done in the first year.
4. **Check the mortgage lender's position.** Some lenders retain money against rating 3 defects until the work is done. Having a quote in hand helps.

## If you're selling

Repointing before you market is worth it if the pointing is visibly bad from the street — buyers form a view of the house before they've got out of the car, and a scruffy gable costs you more in offers than the repointing costs to do. If it's only the survey that would pick it up, most sellers leave it and expect a small negotiation. A chimney that's shedding mortar is the exception: fix it, because it comes up on every survey and every buyer worries about it.

Get it done properly if you do it. A quick smear-over to get through a sale is easy to spot, and a bad surveyor's report on new pointing is worse than one on old.
