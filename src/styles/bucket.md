Bucket handle — also called concave or half-round — is tooled after the mortar has firmed up, by running a round bar or a purpose-made jointer along the joint. The bar compresses the mortar into a smooth curve.

The compression is the point: it densifies the surface and closes it up, so the joint resists weather well. The curve also disguises slight variation in brick size and alignment, which is why it's so common on ordinary 20th-century housing.

Most of the 1930s and post-war semis we repoint in Middleton, Royton and Heywood were originally bucket handle, and we match it.
