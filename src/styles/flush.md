A flush joint is filled right to the face of the masonry and cut off level with a trowel, then usually brushed once it's firmed up to give a slightly textured surface. It's the simplest profile and the most common finish on stone, where the irregular edges of the stones make a tooled profile impractical.

On brick it gives a calm, flat wall with less shadow than a struck or recessed joint. It sheds water reasonably well because there's nowhere for it to sit, but it doesn't have the deliberate run-off angle of a weather struck joint.

Flush is the normal finish for lime pointing on period property. We use it on most of the stone terraces and cottages around Rochdale, Whitworth and Littleborough, brushed back to expose the aggregate and match the original.
