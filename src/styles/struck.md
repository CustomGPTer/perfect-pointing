A weather struck joint is pressed in with the trowel angled so the mortar slopes outward from top to bottom — recessed slightly under the brick above, flush or slightly proud at the brick below. Rain running down the wall hits the slope and is thrown off the face rather than sitting on a ledge.

It's the most durable profile because the top edge of every joint is protected and water can't pool. It also compacts the mortar well as it's tooled, which matters for longevity.

We use weather struck on most exposed cement-mortar work — gables, chimneys, north-facing elevations and boundary walls. On the Pennine side of Rochdale and Oldham it's the sensible default for brick. The variant with the bottom edge cut off cleanly is called weather struck and cut.
