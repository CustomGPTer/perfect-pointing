Tuck pointing is a decorative technique from the 18th and 19th centuries. The joints are first filled with a mortar coloured to match the brick, so the wall reads as one surface. Then a narrow groove is cut and a fine ribbon of white lime putty is pressed in, giving the illusion of very thin, precise joints between perfect bricks.

It's slow, skilled work and it only makes sense where it's the original finish — usually the front elevation of a Victorian or Edwardian villa, a bay, or a shopfront. We restore tuck pointing where a surviving section tells us what the original looked like, matching the stopping mortar colour and the width of the tuck.

You'll see it on some of the older fronts in Rochdale, Bury and north Manchester.
